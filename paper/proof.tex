\begin{abstract}
Observed opinion dispersion can fall because true opinions converge or because
answers become less noisy. We derive the decomposition for repeated multi-item
attitude measures in partially nested and multiple-membership deliberative
designs. Wave-varying measurement error contaminates within-group
homogenization, the intraclass correlation, and regressions of change on prior
opinion. Under classical independent error it cannot, however, manufacture an
increase in the absolute variance of group means. Multiple invariant indicators,
random assignment to groups, and a survey-only control arm identify distinct
estimands for true convergence, between-group divergence, and crystallization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

Let $a\in\{0,1\}$ index control and deliberation, $t\in\{1,2\}$ wave,
$i$ person, $g$ assigned group, and $k$ item. The measurement model is
\begin{equation}
  y_{kita}=\nu_k+\lambda_k\eta_{ita}+\epsilon_{kita},
  \qquad \E(\epsilon_{kita}\mid\eta_{ita},a,g)=0.
\end{equation}
Loadings and intercepts are invariant across waves and arms; residual variances
$\theta_{kta}$ are free. For fixed composite weights $q$ normalized so that
$\sum_k q_k\lambda_k=1$, define
\begin{equation}
  S_{ita}=\sum_k q_k(y_{kita}-\nu_k)=\eta_{ita}+u_{ita},
  \qquad \omega_{ta}=\Var(u_{ita}\mid a).
\end{equation}
The scalar $\omega_{ta}=q'\Theta_{ta}q$ includes residual covariance when it is
modeled. ``Crystallization'' is a treatment-caused reduction in $\omega$.

\section{What changing reliability does and does not bias}

\begin{proposition}[The mean effect survives heteroskedastic measurement error]
If measurement errors have conditional mean zero, then
\[
\E[(S_{i2}-S_{i1})\mid a=1]-\E[(S_{i2}-S_{i1})\mid a=0]
=\E[(\eta_{i2}-\eta_{i1})\mid a=1]-\E[(\eta_{i2}-\eta_{i1})\mid a=0].
\]
Equality holds without requiring $\omega_{1a}=\omega_{2a}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $S_{it}=\eta_{it}+u_{it}$ and apply iterated expectations. Each error
mean is zero, so both arm-specific mean error changes vanish. Different error
variances change the sampling variance, not the estimand.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Observed within-group homogenization mixes two mechanisms]
Under local independence of $u$ across people and independence from true opinion,
the population within-group variance satisfies
\[
W_{tga}(S)=W_{tga}(\eta)+\omega_{ta}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
W_{2g1}(S)-W_{1g1}(S)
=\{W_{2g1}(\eta)-W_{1g1}(\eta)\}+(\omega_{21}-\omega_{11}).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Within a group, $\Var(\eta+u)=\Var(\eta)+\Var(u)+2\Cov(\eta,u)$.
The covariance is zero by assumption. Subtract the two wave-specific identities.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
If true within-group dispersion is unchanged and $\omega_{21}<\omega_{11}$,
observed variance falls. The ratio statistic
$H=1-W_2(S)/W_1(S)$ is positive even though true convergence is zero.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Classical crystallization cannot create absolute divergence]
For balanced groups of size $n$, independent person-level error implies
\[
\Var_g(\bar S_{gt})=\Var_g(\bar\eta_{gt})+\frac{\omega_t}{n}.
\]
Holding true group means fixed, a fall in $\omega_t$ weakly lowers the absolute
variance of observed group means. With unequal sizes the error contribution for
group $g$ is $\omega_t/n_g$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\bar S_{gt}=\bar\eta_{gt}+n^{-1}\sum_i u_{igt}$. Independence makes the cross
covariance zero and the variance of the averaged error $\omega_t/n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This proposition is a useful boundary. Crystallization can create apparent
within-group homogenization, but not an increase in the absolute variance of
group means under the stated assumptions. A claimed absolute divergence artifact
therefore requires nonclassical correlated error, changing group sizes or
composition, scale noninvariance, or a statistic other than absolute variance.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Relative divergence can rise mechanically]
If $\eta_{igt}=\mu_t+b_{gt}+v_{igt}$ with variances $\sigma^2_{bt}$ and
$\sigma^2_{wt}$, then
\[
\operatorname{ICC}_t(S)=
\frac{\sigma^2_{bt}}{\sigma^2_{bt}+\sigma^2_{wt}+\omega_t}.
\]
For $\sigma^2_{bt}>0$, its derivative with respect to $\omega_t$ is negative.
Thus reducing error raises the observed ICC even when both true variance
components are fixed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The group covariance of two distinct people is $\sigma_b^2$, while each observed
variance adds $\omega_t$ to the true total. Differentiate the ratio.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Observed prior opinion induces regression to the mean]
Under classical error independent across waves,
\[
\Cov(S_{i2}-S_{i1},S_{i1})
=\Cov(\eta_{i2}-\eta_{i1},\eta_{i1})-\omega_1.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand the covariance of $(\Delta\eta_i+u_{i2}-u_{i1})$ and
$(\eta_{i1}+u_{i1})$. All cross terms vanish except
$\Cov(-u_{i1},u_{i1})=-\omega_1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Structural decomposition}

For a fixed group, write $x_i=\eta_{i1}-\bar\eta_{g1}$. The treated change model
is
\[
\eta_{i2}=\eta_{i1}+\tau+\gamma(\bar\eta_{g1}-\eta_{i1})+b_g+\delta_i.
\]
After subtracting the T2 group mean,
\[
\eta_{i2}-\bar\eta_{g2}=(1-\gamma)x_i+(\delta_i-\bar\delta_g).
\]
When $x_i$ and $\delta_i$ are independent, the inherited within-group variance is
multiplied by $(1-\gamma)^2$; idiosyncratic true change may offset that reduction.
The common effect $b_g$ cancels within group and contributes to between-group
variance. Hence $\gamma$, $\sigma_b^2$, and $\omega_2-\omega_1$ correspond to
different mechanisms.

With reshuffling, let $W$ contain session weights and $b$ the session effects.
Then
\[
\Var(Wb)=\sigma_b^2WW',
\]
so two participants' group-induced changes covary in proportion to the overlap
of their session weights. One-hot rows recover fixed groups.

\section{Identification}

With at least three locally independent indicators and a scale constraint,
off-diagonal item covariances identify the common factor variance because
$\Cov(y_k,y_\ell)=\lambda_k\lambda_\ell\Var(\eta)$ for $k\ne\ell$.
Indicator variances then identify residual variances. Equality of loadings and
intercepts across waves makes the latent scale comparable; equality of residual
variances is not imposed and is precisely the restriction being studied
\citep{mcardle2009}. Same-item residual correlations across waves must be modeled
or tested rather than silently set to zero.

Random assignment to groups makes baseline latent composition independent of
group effects. A survey-only control arm identifies the error-variance change
from retesting and panel conditioning. The crystallization estimand is
\[
C=(\omega_{21}-\omega_{11})-(\omega_{20}-\omega_{10}).
\]
Interpreting $C$ causally additionally requires measurement invariance, no
differential attrition, and no treatment-induced reporting process omitted from
the measurement model. Single-item outcomes observed twice do not identify both
latent change and two error variances without further information.

The design is partially nested because treatment participants share deliberation
groups while controls do not \citep{bauer2008,roberts2013}. For multiple
sessions, the multiple-membership extension has covariance
\[
\sigma_b^2WW'.
\]

\section{Implications}

Variance-based claims in deliberative polling should distinguish three targets:
true within-group convergence, absolute between-group divergence, and reduced
measurement error. The first and third are inseparable from a single noisy index.
The second is not mechanically inflated by classical error reduction, although an
ICC or between/within ratio is. This distinction narrows the model's burden and
clarifies what a reanalysis of the twenty-one-poll archive can and cannot revise
\citep{luskin2022}.
